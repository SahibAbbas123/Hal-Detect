## 1. Why a paired test, and why the exact binomial

Every routed-vs-baseline figure in this project is two point estimates with a Wilson CI on each
arm **separately**. But the two systems run on **the same claims**, so their errors are paired
and the arms are **not independent**. Two overlapping CIs do not show the systems are alike, and
two disjoint ones overstate the gap. McNemar's test uses only the claims where the two systems
**disagree**, which is the only place information about a difference lives.

**Exact binomial, not χ².** The χ² approximation to McNemar is invalid below roughly 25
discordant pairs. Every population here has between **0 and 24**. Using χ² would report a
spuriously small `p` in exactly the regime where this project has least data — the same class of
error as quoting a bare 100%.

### 1.1 The power floor — the single most useful thing this test produced

With `n` discordant pairs the smallest two-sided exact `p` obtainable is

```
min_p(n) = 2 / 2^n        reached only when every discordant pair falls one way
```

| n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | **6** | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `min_p` | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0.250 | **0.1250** | 0.0625 | **0.03125** | 0.0156 |

`min_discordant_for(0.05) == 6`. **Below 6 discordant pairs, α = 0.05 is not reachable no matter
how lopsided the result.**

This converts the headline finding from a soft statement into a hard one. At n = 4, `p = 0.1250`
is **not** "the systems are similar" and **not** "routing failed to prove itself" — it is the
**best possible outcome the design admits**, and a perfect 4–0 sweep achieved it. The number is a
statement about **corpus size**, not about the systems. Every `p` in this document is therefore
printed beside its `min_p`, and any row where `p == min_p` is labelled **UNDERPOWERED** rather
than "not significant".

This is the same discipline as R4 (never quote accuracy without coverage and effective n),
applied to significance: **never quote a `p` without the smallest `p` its `n` could have
produced.**

## 3. Three scoring conventions, because "correct" is ambiguous under abstention

Both systems can answer TRUE, FALSE, or abstain. There is no single right way to score a paired
comparison between two abstaining systems, and **picking one silently would choose the result.**
So all three run, always, side by side:

| | definition | the question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **C1 both-covered** | restrict to rows where **both** systems emit a confident verdict; correct = exact match | *At equal willingness to answer, who is right?* |
| **C2 exact-match** | three-valued prediction == three-valued gold, on all paired rows | *Who is right overall — abstaining on a gold TRUE is an error.* Charges routing for its coverage. |
| **C3 confident-error** | correct = did **not** emit a confident wrong verdict; abstention is not an error | *Who avoids the one output class a detector must never produce?* |

**They answer three different questions and they disagree** — that disagreement is a finding, not
noise. C1 is the fairest head-to-head and the weakest in power. C2 is the harshest on routing. C3
is the most flattering to routing and, as §5 shows, **gamed**.

**R7 lives inside `correct()`.** It is plain `pred == gold`, deliberately *not* "abstention is
always wrong": predicting UNVERIFIABLE on a **gold**-UNVERIFIABLE claim is **right**, and a
confident verdict on one is an **error**. Gold UNVERIFIABLE (not checkable in principle) and
predicted UNVERIFIABLE (could not reach evidence) must never be merged, and the place that
distinction bites hardest is exactly here.

**Unmatched rows are dropped, never defaulted.** A claim the baseline never scored is not a claim
the baseline got wrong; defaulting it to UNVERIFIABLE would **invent** a discordant pair. The
pairing also asserts that both files agree on the gold label for every joined `claim_id`, and
raises rather than silently preferring one.

**Every degenerate strategy is run against the same baseline under the same convention**
(R3 extended to a significance test): Always-True, Always-False, Always-UNVERIFIABLE. A `GAMED`
flag fires when a degenerate arm reaches a `p` at least as small as the real system's, in the
same direction.

## 4. QUANTITATIVE — the combined 53

**Population prior (R6):** n = 53, gold **28 TRUE / 25 FALSE / 0 UNVERIFIABLE**,
π_FALSE = **47.2%**. Near-balanced, by construction — this is the natural Batch-3 29 plus the 24
QUANTITATIVE synthetic negatives.

`b` = routed right, baseline wrong. `c` = routed wrong, baseline right. `b > c` favours routing.

| convention | operating point | b | c | n | `p` | `min_p` | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** both-covered | @shipped (0.50/0.40) | **4** | 0 | 4 | **0.1250** | 0.1250 | **UNDERPOWERED — `p` is at the floor** |
| **C1** both-covered | @tuned (0.35/0.30) | **6** | 0 | 6 | **0.0312** | 0.0312 | significant, favours routing, **not gamed** — **but see §4.2, do not quote it** |
| **C2** exact-match | @shipped | 6 | **17** | 23 | **0.0347** | 2.4e-07 | **significant, favours the BASELINE** |
| **C2** exact-match | @tuned | 6 | **18** | 24 | **0.0227** | 1.2e-07 | **significant, favours the BASELINE** |
| **C3** conf-error | @shipped | **19** | 0 | 19 | 3.81e-06 | 3.81e-06 | favours routing — **GAMED** |
| **C3** conf-error | @tuned | **24** | 0 | 24 | 1.19e-07 | 1.19e-07 | favours routing — **GAMED** |

### 4.1 C1 @shipped: the 4–0 is underpowered *by construction*

The four discordant pairs are `SYN_QUAN_010`, `011`, `012`, `013` — gold FALSE, routed FALSE,
baseline TRUE, all won by routing. These are the Mount Everest / Aconcagua / Denali / Kilimanjaro
elevation perturbations already documented in
`reference/rq2_quantitative_negatives_2026-08-25.md` §2.

Routing swept every pair. It still cannot reach α = 0.05, because n = 4 and the floor is 0.1250.
**The right sentence is "the design cannot resolve this difference", not "the difference is not
there".** Reaching significance needs **6** discordant pairs — which on this corpus means more
gold negatives, not a better verifier. **14 gold negatives across the natural 356 is the binding
constraint on every detection claim in the thesis**, and here it binds on the *test statistic*
rather than on the estimate.

### 4.2 C1 @tuned reaches p = 0.0312 — and it must not be quoted. It is manufactured.

Two rows separate n = 4 from n = 6, and **verified, not assumed**, they are not what a reader
would guess. Two movements happen at the tuned point and only one of them matters:

| movement | rows | effect on the test |
|---|---|---|
| (i) baseline **starts answering** | `B3_04_C02`, `B3_04_C10` — gold TRUE, routed TRUE, baseline `UNVERIFIABLE → TRUE` | both **concordant**; contribute **nothing** |
| (ii) baseline **gets worse** | `SYN_QUAN_014`, `SYN_QUAN_015` — gold FALSE, routed FALSE, baseline `FALSE → TRUE` | were **concordant-correct**, become **discordant, routing wins** |

So the two extra discordant pairs come from the baseline **flipping two rows it previously got
right into two rows it gets wrong.** Routing's predictions are **byte-identical at both operating
points** — the routed verifier has no threshold at all. Confident errors go **19 → 24**.

**The significance is purchased by handing the baseline its worst operating point on this
population.** CLAUDE.md §4 already rules the tuned point a **reverse strawman** on
negatives-enriched populations (from `rq2_quantitative_negatives_2026-08-25.md` §6: F1(FALSE)
0.488 → 0.200, MCC +0.202 → +0.083). This test now shows the *significance* has the same defect
as the *point estimate*, and the mechanism is measured rather than argued.

**Do not quote `p = 0.0312`.** The admissible C1 headline is @shipped's **underpowered
`p = 0.1250`**. Recorded here in full precisely so nobody rediscovers the tuned number later and
mistakes it for a win.

> **Generalisable methodological point, and it is new to this project:** *statistical power in a
> paired test is a function of the opponent's operating point.* Degrading the comparator
> manufactures discordant pairs. Any threshold chosen after seeing a `p` is a `p` chosen after
> seeing a threshold.

### 4.3 C2 significantly favours the baseline — the claim that got weaker

Under exact-match, routing's abstentions count as errors, and there are 39 of them (73.6%
abstention rate). `c = 17` @shipped: seventeen rows the baseline answers correctly and routing
declines to answer. `p = 0.0347`, `p = 0.0227` @tuned.

This is **real and it should be reported.** Routing's coverage cost is statistically significant
on a paired test; its accuracy advantage is not. Both facts come from the same 53 rows and the
same code path.

It is also the convention whose *premise* is most contestable — C2 charges an abstention on a
gold TRUE as a full error, which is precisely the accounting an abstaining system is designed to
reject (R-Tuning's risk-coverage framing, cited in CLAUDE.md §7, exists to reject it). **Report
C2 as the coverage cost measured, not as a verdict on routing.** But report it.

### 4.4 C3 is gamed by abstention — so "0 vs 19" cannot carry a `p`

`b = 19, c = 0, p = 3.81e-06` looks like the strongest result in the document. It is worthless as
a significance claim: **Always-UNVERIFIABLE scores the identical `b = 19, c = 0, p = 3.81e-06`**,
because a system that never answers can never emit a confident error.

This is the significance-test mirror of the Always-False `F1(FALSE) = 0.641` trap (R3) and of
Always-True's 87.9% accuracy. The **count** 0-vs-19 remains a true and useful statement about
error profiles — CLAUDE.md's RQ2 verdict already leans on exactly this and is unaffected. What
cannot be attached to it is a `p`-value, because the null it rejects is one that a verifier which
does nothing at all also rejects.

### 4.5 Negatives-only populations: McNemar is INADMISSIBLE

Run @shipped on the negatives alone, both cache tiers (`reference/negatives_cache_audit_2026-08-25.md`):

| population | π_FALSE | conv | b | c | `p` | gamed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cache-clean 15 | 100% | C1 | 4 | 0 | 0.1250 | **Always-False, identical b=4 c=0** |
| cache-clean 15 | 100% | C2 | 4 | 7 | 0.5488 | Always-False |
| cache-clean 15 | 100% | C3 | 4 | 0 | 0.1250 | **Always-False *and* Always-UNVERIFIABLE** |
| independent 14 | 100% | C1 | 4 | 0 | 0.1250 | Always-False |
| independent 14 | 100% | C2 | 4 | 6 | 0.7539 | Always-False |
| independent 14 | 100% | C3 | 4 | 0 | 0.1250 | Always-False |

**On an all-gold-FALSE population an unconditional "FALSE" is correct on every row, so it wins
every discordant pair the routed system wins, under every convention.** A paired significance
test on a single-class population cannot distinguish a detector from a constant.

This is **R5** — *never quote a detection rate on negatives alone* — reappearing as a
significance-test result. It is now a rule in its own right: **McNemar requires a population with
both gold classes present.** The combined 53 (§4) qualifies; the negatives alone never do.

## 5. CITATION Level 1b — the paired test is the wrong instrument, and that is the finding

**Populations (R6).** All 53 = 24 TRUE / 28 FALSE / 1 UNVERIFIABLE, π_FALSE **52.8%**; negatives
only 24, π_FALSE **100%**; natural only 29 = 24 TRUE / 4 FALSE / 1 UNVERIFIABLE, π_FALSE
**13.8%**. (`citation_l1b_results.csv` is 54 rows; 53 after `SYN_CITA_083` is filtered out by
`DROPPED`, with the row still on disk as evidence.)

| population | conv | b | c | n | `p` | `min_p` | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all 53 | **C1** | 1 | 0 | **1** | **1.0** | 1.0 | **one discordant pair — no result is possible** |
| all 53 | C2 | 7 | 11 | 18 | 0.4807 | 7.6e-06 | **INADMISSIBLE** + gamed (Always-True, Always-False) |
| all 53 | C3 | 9 | 0 | 9 | 0.0039 | 0.0039 | favours L1b — **GAMED** (Always-UNVERIFIABLE identical) |
| negatives 24 | C1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1.0 | 1.0 | underpowered, **gamed** (Always-False) |
| negatives 24 | C2 | 6 | 2 | 8 | 0.2891 | 0.0078 | **INADMISSIBLE** + gamed |
| negatives 24 | C3 | 5 | 0 | 5 | 0.0625 | 0.0625 | underpowered, **gamed** ×2 |
| natural 29 | **C1** | 0 | 0 | **0** | **1.0** | 1.0 | **NO DISCORDANT PAIRS AT ALL** |
| natural 29 | C2 | 1 | 9 | 10 | **0.0215** | 0.0020 | significant, favours the baseline — but **INADMISSIBLE** + gamed |
| natural 29 | C3 | 4 | 0 | 4 | 0.1250 | 0.1250 | underpowered, **gamed** |

**Every C2 row here is inadmissible, and for a structural reason rather than a statistical one:
L1b cannot return TRUE by design** (it widens spans and abstains; `series_resolver` makes no TRUE
verdict, and Level 1 owns TRUE). Under exact-match it therefore forfeits every gold-TRUE row
automatically — on the natural 29, where 24 of 29 rows are gold TRUE, that alone produces
`c = 9`. Scoring a FALSE-only component on a three-valued exact match measures its **design**, not
its performance; the row is printed because omitting it would look like hiding it, and the module
flags it in-line for the same reason. **C2 becomes meaningful for CITATION only when L1 and L1b
are scored as one stack**, which is not what this comparison is.

Note the natural-29 C2 row is *also* gamed by Always-True (`p = 6.10e-05`, favouring A) — on a
population that is 83% gold TRUE, an unconditional TRUE beats the baseline outright. That is R3's
Always-True trap, restated as a `p`-value.

### 5.1 Why C1's n is 1: the two systems answer on different claims

C1 n = 1 is consistent with "small overlap" or with "large overlap, near-total agreement". It is
the former, decisively. **Verified cross-tab over all 53:**

| | baseline FALSE | baseline TRUE | baseline abstains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1b FALSE** | 3 | 1 | **5** |
| **L1b abstains** | 5 | 14 | 25 |

- L1b covers **9**; the baseline covers **23**; the **union is 28** and the **intersection is 4**.
- The paired test can only see those 4 rows. Three are concordant (both FALSE, both right); one
  is discordant (`SYN_CITA_075`, L1b right). Head-to-head on the overlap: L1b **4/4**, baseline
  **3/4**. Nothing separable.

**The systems reach different evidence.** L1b asks Wikidata for a work's `P577`/`P123`; the
baseline asks BART-MNLI about retrieved Wikipedia passages. Their reachability sets are close to
disjoint, so a head-to-head is not merely underpowered — **it is answering a question these two
components do not pose to each other.**

On the natural 29 the overlap is **zero**, because L1b emits no confident verdict there at all
(its FPR is 0/24 and its true positives on the natural set are 0 by construction, all 4 gold-FALSE
natural CITATION claims being content falsities gated out — CLAUDE.md §3).

### 5.2 The composition table — what each system uniquely contributes

This is the number the head-to-head was hiding, and it is the more useful one:

| region | n | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| both answer | 4 | all gold FALSE; L1b 4/4, baseline 3/4 |
| **only L1b answers** | **5** | all gold FALSE, **L1b correct on 5/5** — `SYN_CITA_063`, `064`, `067`, `076`, `077` |
| only the baseline answers | 19 | **11 right / 8 wrong** (9 TRUE-right, 2 FALSE-right; 5 FALSE-wrong, 2 TRUE-wrong, 1 gold-UNVERIFIABLE-wrong) |
| neither answers | 25 | 12 gold FALSE, 13 gold TRUE — the shared ceiling |

**L1b adds 5 correct detections on rows the baseline cannot reach, at zero cost in confident
errors.** That is the **hybrid fall-through (#3)** argument made quantitative for the first time,
and it is grounded in the literature by ConfQA's *"unsure as a gate that triggers retrieval"*
(CLAUDE.md §7). The right framing for CITATION is **"do these compose?"** — measurably yes — and
not **"which one wins?"**, which this corpus cannot answer and which mischaracterises the
components.

**Caveats travel unchanged (R11).** 8 of L1b's 9 detections still come from **Q103843442** alone,
and the 5 unique detections are inside that concentration. The composition finding is about
*coverage disjointness*, which the concentration does not affect; the *magnitude* 5 is as thin as
every other CITATION number.

## 6. What this changes, and what it does not

**Changed — write these into the thesis:**

1. The QUANTITATIVE **4–0** is **underpowered by construction** (`p = 0.1250 = min_p` at n = 4).
   Quote it as an error-profile observation, never as a demonstrated difference.
2. The **0-vs-19 confident-error gap** cannot carry a `p`, because Always-UNVERIFIABLE scores the
   same one. The count stands; the significance framing does not.
3. Routing's **coverage cost is significant** (C2, `p = 0.0347` / `0.0227`), favouring the
   baseline. This belongs in the RQ2 tradeoff table beside the accuracy figures.
4. For **CITATION**, replace the head-to-head with the **composition** table: union 28 vs
   intersection 4, and L1b's 5 unique correct detections.
5. **Two new admissibility rules**: McNemar needs both gold classes (§4.5), and a confident-error
   metric is gamed by abstention (§4.4).

**Unchanged — this test does not touch:**

- **MCC +0.378 vs +0.202.** Not a paired statistic; nothing here bears on it.
- Coverage, selective accuracy, and every Wilson CI in the existing docs.
- L1b's **FPR 0/24**, its **0 confident errors**, and the **J +0.360 / +0.375** comparison — all
  unpaired marginal statistics.
- The threshold-sweep surface, the base-rate argument, and Youden's J = +0.322.

**The honest summary.** Routing's advantage on QUANTITATIVE is visible in the error profile and
in MCC but is **not statistically demonstrable on 53 rows with 25 negatives**. The corpus, not
the verifier, is the limiting factor — and the test's own power floor is the cleanest evidence
for that claim the project has produced. **A thesis that reports this is stronger than one that
reports `p < 0.05` from the tuned operating point**, which §4.2 shows to be an artifact of
degrading the comparator.

## 7. Scope limit — there is no whole-system McNemar over the natural 356

The routed system is not a single artifact on the natural set: **TEMPORAL and EA sit at 0%
coverage** (last-measured, pre-port), and **RELATIONAL's live logic is notebook cell 57 with no
committed per-claim CSV**. So the test runs **per type**, on the two types with a committed
routed result file and both gold classes present.

**A per-type test cannot be pooled into a system-level claim** — the types have different priors,
different coverage, and different verifiers, and pooling them would reintroduce exactly the
prior-mixing R6 forbids. A system-level paired test becomes possible only after #19 produces
committed TEMPORAL/EA per-claim results and RELATIONAL's cell-57 logic is extracted to a `.py`
with a saved CSV.
