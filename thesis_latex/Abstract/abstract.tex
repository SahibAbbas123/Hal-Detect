Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate fluent and authoritative assertions that contradict real-world factual ground truth. Existing verification frameworks predominantly treat fact-checking as an undifferentiated neural text-matching task, rendering them vulnerable to lexical overlap bias, continuous attention dilution, and prohibitive compute costs. 

In this dissertation, we propose \textbf{HalDetect}, a type-directed neuro-symbolic verification framework that reformulates fact-checking as a modality-triage problem. HalDetect decomposes generated prose into typed atomic 5-tuples $\langle S, P, O, T, M \rangle$, resolves paragraph-level pronoun coreference chains in $< 0.1\text{ms}$, and routes assertions via a high-speed deterministic classifier ($< 0.05\text{ms}$) to five specialized Arithmetic Logic Units (ALUs): a Dimensional SI Quantitative ALU, a 7-Continent Topological Superlative Resolver, an Entity-Attribute Statement Matcher, a CrossRef Citation Gateway, and a calibrated Relational NLI fallback.

Evaluated on a balanced 1,000-claim benchmark (500 gold \texttt{TRUE} and 500 gold \texttt{FALSE} assertions), HalDetect achieves \textbf{97.60\% selective accuracy} ($95\%$ CI: $[96.45\%, 98.38\%]$) and a Matthews Correlation Coefficient of $\mathbf{+0.9520}$, outperforming Modern SOTA RAG ($69.20\%$) and non-routed baselines ($60.00\%$) with high statistical significance (paired McNemar $\chi^2 = 278.09, p < 10^{-60}$). In an open-ended road test spanning 1,798 propositions generated by seven foundation models (Qwen 2.5, Mistral, DeepSeek-R1, Gemma 31B, GPT-OSS 120B, MiniMax M3), HalDetect achieves \textbf{100.0\% recall on real-world hallucinations} (catching 31 out of 31 errors), whereas neural RAG and MiniCheck-7B achieve 0.0\% recall due to token overlap blindness. HalDetect verifies claims in \textbf{24.3 ms on a consumer CPU} with zero GPU memory, establishing that neuro-symbolic triage provides a scalable and mathematically sound foundation for reliable AI fact-checking.
